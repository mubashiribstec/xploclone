## G. Conversational / natural range (read naturally — NOT acted drama)

A monotone clean read produces a monotone clone. Vary your pace and
emphasis the way you would on a real call: pause where you would
pause, stress the word that matters, let questions rise. Do not
over-enunciate and do not perform — this is your normal speaking
voice in four everyday modes.

## H. Long-form passage (2+ minutes — read in ONE take, do not stop)

Read this in one continuous take, at your normal speaking pace, with

55. Let me tell you how a normal day goes, because it's never quite what people expect. I usually get up around half six, make coffee, and check my messages before the house wakes up. There's always something waiting — a candidate asking about a shift, a manager asking for an update, a file that needs converting before nine. I deal with the quick ones first, then I sit down properly and work through the rest. Around ten, the calls start. Some are short, just confirming a time or a postcode. Some are longer, and those are the ones I enjoy most, because that's when you actually get to know someone — what they've done, what they're good at, what kind of work they really want. I take notes as I go, because I never trust myself to remember the details later. By lunchtime I've usually spoken to six or seven people, and there's a list on my desk of who needs what. The afternoon is for follow-ups: sending details by email, booking interviews, chasing the one document that's always missing. It's not glamorous work, but it matters, and when someone calls back a week later to say they got the job, that's the whole point. That's why I do it. And honestly, the best calls are the ones where the person on the other end relaxes halfway through — you can hear it in their voice, the moment they stop reading from a script and start talking like themselves. That's when you know you've got the right person for the role.
